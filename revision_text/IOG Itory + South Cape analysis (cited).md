## 1) Translation/localization notes (Chapter 06 Revision — Part 1)

### 1.1 Extended shouts: closest romanization vs natural English

#### Line 29: カレン： ビルおじいさまーっ！
- **What’s being extended in Japanese**: the long-vowel mark **ー** lengthens the final syllable of **おじいさま** (specifically the **ま → まー**), and the terminal small **っ** is a sharp cutoff/glottal stop.
- **Closest romanization (effect-focused)**: *Biru ojii-samaa!* (elongation is on the honorific, not on “Bill”).
- **Natural English**: English typically elongates the **name** in a call (“Biiill!”), not the word “grandfather/grandpa,” so localizations often shift the stretch to the name for readability.

#### Line 30: テム： ローラおばあちーゃん！
- **What’s being extended in Japanese**: the long mark lengthens **ち** in **ちーゃん**, producing an elongated **-chan** (≈ *chaan*), not an elongated “Laura.”
- **Closest romanization (effect-focused)**: *Rōra obaa-chaan!* (elongation is on the suffix, not on “Laura”).

### 1.2 Karen’s scream: キャーーーーーーッ！！！ … た、たいへん、たいへんよ！

#### “Kyaaaah” vs “Aaaah” (tone/register choice)
- **Japanese**: キャーーーーーーッ is the conventional written scream (**キャ** + long mark + cutoff **ッ**).
- **Localization guidance** (no single universal rule): using **“Kyaaaah”** preserves a recognizable Japanese scream convention (but can read “anime-ish” in English); **“Aaaah!”** preserves the panic without importing that convention.

#### たいへん (taihen): “terrible” vs “dreadful” vs “disaster”
- **Functional meaning in this line**: a common panic exclamation meaning roughly “this is bad/terrible,” intensified by stutter and repetition.
- **Recommendation used in-session**: **“terrible”** is the closest, most neutral-register English equivalent; “dreadful” skews more formal/old-fashioned; “disaster” intensifies and narrows the meaning to a single catastrophic event.

## 2) Itory Village: genealogy, depiction, and “how related are Will and Lilly likely to be?”

### 2.1 Game-plot anchors we relied on
From the walkthrough summary of Itory Village:
- Lilly brings Will (and Kara) to Itory Village; the houses are initially invisible until “Lola’s Melody” is played.
- In the graveyard, an elder’s spirit explains Will’s mother **Shira** is the only child of **Bill and Lola**, and that Shira made the barrier that protects the village.

Source: Thonky walkthrough, “Itory Village” (`https://www.thonky.com/illusion-of-gaia/itory-village`).

### 2.2 Your in-game population observations and what they imply
- **South Cape (your count)**: ~20–30 NPCs across ~5 households/families; ~4–5 small dwellings + 1 large wealthy house + church/school.
- **Itory (your count)**: ~10–15 NPCs; 3 visible houses (Lilly’s + 2 others), with an implied additional home for Bill/Lola/Shira in backstory.

### 2.3 Converting “NPC count” to plausible household counts (historical benchmark)
To ground headcounts, we used a widely cited early-modern household size benchmark:
- **Mean household size** in England/Wales was relatively constant at about **4.75 persons (or slightly below)** from the **16th century until 1911**, based on pre-census community listings analyzed by the Cambridge Group for the History of Population and Social Structure.

Source: Laslett (summary via PubMed abstract): `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22077606/`.

Practical implication:
- **South Cape**: 20–30 people ≈ ~4–7 “average” households at ~4.75 persons/household.
- **Itory**: 10–15 people ≈ ~2–3 households; if you treat Bill/Lola’s implied house as distinct, ~3–4 households.

### 2.4 Why “some cousin-degree related” is the default in a tiny, closed community
We used a basic genealogy constraint:
- In any finite population, you can’t keep doubling distinct ancestors forever; eventually ancestry overlaps (the same individuals appear multiple times in a pedigree). This is commonly called **pedigree collapse**.

Source: Wikipedia overview: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedigree_collapse`.

Implication for Itory:
- If Itory is genuinely **tiny** and **endogamous** for multiple generations, “unrelated” pairs become increasingly likely to share ancestors within a modest number of generations.
- Will is explicitly descended from an Itory line via Shira/Bill/Lola (Thonky), so Will↔Lilly sharing ancestry becomes even more likely than two random outsiders.

## 3) “South Cape as a colonial town” — plausible real-world analogues and ranges

### 3.1 What “South Cape” resembles given your count
With ~20–30 people, South Cape is best described as a **hamlet/outpost**, not a port city, even if it has docks.

### 3.2 Example of a frontier military/administrative settlement with cited headcount
To show that “strategic settlements” can be anchored around defense/admin (not necessarily a bustling port), we used a Spanish frontier presidio example:
- **San Antonio de Béxar Presidio** (founded 1718): in **1726** it had **45 soldiers**, and the **total Spanish population was estimated at 200** including families/nearby settlers.

Source: Texas State Historical Association entry: `https://tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/san-antonio-de-bexar-presidio`.

Note: this example is *still much larger* than South Cape’s 20–30, reinforcing that South Cape is extremely small by historical standards.

### 3.3 Institutional model: mission + presidio + pueblo (why “church/school” fits)
We cited a general overview of Spanish Borderlands settlement institutions:
- Spain used three basic institutions: **mission**, **presidio**, and **pueblo**; frontier regions often relied heavily on missions/presidios rather than large commercial towns.

Source: Digital History (University of Houston), “The Spanish Borderlands” overview: `https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?psid=528&smtID=3`.

## 4) Itory’s “Inca/Andes” signal: how close are the Incas to Peru, and does “mountainous” fit?

### 4.1 Core geographic fact
- **Cusco (Cuzco/Cusco)** is in **south-central Peru**, stands high in the **Andes**, and was the capital of the Inca empire (Tawantinsuyu).

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Cuzco”: `http://www.britannica.com/place/Cuzco`.

### 4.2 Empire extent (for “vague-but-recognizable” parallels)
Britannica summarizes Tawantinsuyu’s late-15th-century reach broadly across modern national boundaries (toward Ecuador, into central Chile, and into parts of Bolivia/Argentina).

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Cuzco”: `http://www.britannica.com/place/Cuzco`.

Wikipedia’s overview also frames the Inca Empire as Andean/western South American with Cusco as capital.

Source: Wikipedia, “Inca Empire”: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire`.

### 4.3 “Mountainous more than foresty”
Given Cusco’s high-Andes setting (Britannica), “mountainous” environmental cues align well with an Inca/Andean reference set.

## 5) King Edward as an Age-of-Discovery patron: “Spain?” as a plausible (but not exclusive) parallel

The “monarch commissions/sponsors expeditions” pattern is not unique to Spain, but Spain is a strong candidate parallel because crown-backed exploration is central to the era’s popular history.

### 5.1 Spain: crown-sponsored voyages (Columbus example)
Britannica describes the first voyage’s outfitting and financing in Spain, noting:
- ships fitted out at Palos (Spain)
- expedition funding assembled by a royal treasury official and financiers, with Columbus contributing as well
- the “Isabella pawned her jewels” story is identified as a myth

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Christopher Columbus — The first voyage”: `http://www.britannica.com/biography/Christopher-Columbus/The-first-voyage`.

### 5.2 Portugal: elite/royal patronage (Henry the Navigator as patron figure)
Portugal’s early maritime exploration is likewise strongly associated with elite/royal sponsorship (Henry as organizer/patron, whether or not he personally sailed).

Source: Wikipedia, “Henry the Navigator”: `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator`.

### 5.3 Interpretation statement used in-session
So, “Edward resembles a Spain-like ruler” is reasonable if you frame it as:
- **Edward resembles an Age-of-Discovery-style monarch/patron**, with particularly strong overlap with **Iberian** models (Spain/Portugal), but the game remains intentionally **vague-but-recognizable**.
